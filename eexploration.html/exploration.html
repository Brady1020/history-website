Age of Exploration & Revolution

1600 – 1700: Global Horizons and Scientific Discovery

Overview

The 17th century was a time of global expansion and intellectual revolution. European powers established permanent colonies in the Americas and Asia, creating the first truly global trade networks. Meanwhile, the Scientific Revolution transformed how humanity understood the natural world, as figures like Galileo and Newton applied mathematical principles to physics and astronomy. It was also the era of the "Sun King" Louis XIV and the rise of absolute monarchies.

Interactive Timeline

Important Events

  • 1607: Founding of Jamestown, the first permanent English settlement in North America.
  • 1610: Galileo publishes "Sidereus Nuncius," describing his telescopic observations.
  • 1648: The Peace of Westphalia ends the Thirty Years' War and establishes the modern state system.
  • 1687: Isaac Newton publishes "Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica."

Key Historical Figures

  • Galileo Galilei: The "father of modern science" who faced the Inquisition for his views.
  • Isaac Newton: Physicist who formulated the laws of motion and universal gravitation.
  • Louis XIV: The "Sun King" of France, the longest-reigning sovereign in European history.
  • John Locke: Philosopher whose ideas on natural rights influenced future revolutions.

Cultural Impact

The 17th century laid the groundwork for the modern world. The Scientific Revolution established the empirical method as the standard for seeking truth. Colonization led to the "Columbian Exchange," a massive transfer of plants, animals, and cultures between the Old and New Worlds, but also initiated centuries of exploitation and conflict. The philosophical shifts of the late 1600s would soon lead into the Age of Enlightenment.
