The Late Middle Ages

1400 – 1500: A Century of Transition

Overview

The 15th century was a period of profound change in Europe. As the feudal systems of the Middle Ages began to wane, new technologies and political shifts paved the way for the Renaissance. This century witnessed the conclusion of the Hundred Years' War, the fall of the Byzantine Empire, and the revolutionary invention of the movable-type printing press, which democratized knowledge and literacy across the continent.

Interactive Timeline

Important Events

  • 1431: The execution of Joan of Arc in Rouen, France.
  • 1453: The Fall of Constantinople to the Ottoman Empire, ending the Byzantine Empire.
  • 1455: Johannes Gutenberg completes the Gutenberg Bible, the first major book printed with movable type.
  • 1492: Christopher Columbus reaches the Americas, and the Reconquista ends in Spain.

Key Historical Figures

  • Joan of Arc: French heroine who led armies during the Hundred Years' War.
  • Johannes Gutenberg: Inventor of the mechanical movable-type printing press.
  • Mehmed the Conqueror: Ottoman Sultan who captured Constantinople.
  • Leonardo da Vinci: Born in 1452, he represents the transition to the Renaissance.

Cultural Impact

The cultural impact of this century cannot be overstated. The printing press allowed for the rapid dissemination of ideas, which would later fuel the Reformation and the Scientific Revolution. Politically, the rise of centralized monarchies in France, England, and Spain began to replace the fragmented feudal lordships of the earlier medieval period.
